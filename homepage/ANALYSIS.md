**Method note.** bladehq.com is blocked by this environment's network policy, so
this is built from their public pages in search results, their Blade Club
documentation, and their review profiles — not a live crawl. Where a claim
matters, the source is named. Verify anything you plan to act on directly.

## The finding worth acting on today

**Your return policy beats theirs, and you are burying it.**

Blade HQ charges for returns unless a customer has spent enough to reach the
Zirconium tier. You give **every** customer a 30-day window with a **prepaid**
label, emailed to them.

That is a better offer than the category leader, for 100% of buyers instead of
a fraction of them. On your homepage it is currently a small icon in a five-item
strip that reads "30 Days Return".

Say it properly. It costs nothing and it answers the exact fear a first-time
buyer has about an unfamiliar knife shop.

**Second: your free shipping threshold is invisible.** Blade HQ leads with $99.
Your trust bar says "Free Shipping" with no number. A stated threshold is not
a trust badge, it is an **average-order-value lever** — people add items to
reach it. Yours should say the number.

---

## Copy now — cheap, works at your scale

**1. Star ratings on product cards.** Blade HQ carries thousands of reviews and
shows them everywhere. Your cards show none. This is already the number one item
in the game plan; the homepage is where it is most visible.

**2. State the free shipping threshold.** "Free shipping over $50", not "Free
Shipping". Directly lifts AOV.

**3. Lead the returns promise.** "30-day returns — we email you a prepaid
label." Better than the market leader, said plainly.

**4. Shop by price.** Blade HQ is weak here and it suits a value-positioned
store: Under $25 · $25–50 · $50–100 · $100+. Cheap to build, matches how
budget-conscious buyers actually shop, and gives you four indexable pages with
real search volume.

**5. Fix the category cards.** Yours are four tiles with truncated labels —
"Camping and S…", "Hunting Knife S…" — and one, "Locking Knives", is not how
anyone shops. Six to eight clear categories instead.

**6. Surface the blog.** You publish daily and none of it appears on the
homepage. Blade HQ built "Knife Life" into a brand. You do not need that; you
need three guide links so the content earns internal links and returns visits.

**7. Make the hero seasonal, not a brand banner.** Archery opens 1 October —
30 days out. A dated seasonal hero converts better than a product photo, and
it swaps to gifting in November.

## Copy later — needs volume first

**Loyalty programme.** Blade Club is well built, but points reward *repeat*
purchase. At roughly three orders a month, repeat rate is not your constraint —
first purchase is. Revisit at 50+ orders a month.

**Deep mega-menu.** Theirs works because they carry 40,000 SKUs and have the
authority to rank the resulting pages. Building that structure now recreates the
crawled-not-indexed problem.

## Do not copy

**"Biggest selection" positioning.** You run the same wholesale feed as a
hundred other dealers. You cannot win on selection and should not claim it.

**Their scale of content.** A YouTube operation is a multi-year investment.
Three focused guides beat a thin imitation of Knife Life.

---

## What you have that they do not

- **Michigan identity.** Blade HQ is a national warehouse. You are a Michigan
  shop shipping to Michigan hunters in Michigan deer season. That is a story
  they structurally cannot tell, and it is currently absent from your homepage.
- **Free returns for everyone**, as above.
- **Twelve state pages** telling buyers what is legal where they live. No
  competitor on this feed does that.
