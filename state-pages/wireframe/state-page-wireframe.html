02

What changes on mobile

Sticky bottom bar

Fades in after block 02, persists to the footer. One line, one button: “Shop Michigan-legal knives.” Roughly 60–70% of this traffic will be mobile — this is the highest-value single element on the template.

The glance table stacks

It must never scroll horizontally. Each row becomes question above, verdict pill below. If it side-scrolls on a phone, the page’s core asset is broken for most of its audience.

Product grids go 2-up

Four across becomes two across, not a carousel. Carousels bury everything past slide one and measure terribly on this kind of page.

Capture moves up

Block 11 jumps above block 10 on mobile. Scroll depth falls off faster on phones, so the capture has to come before the review wall.

04

Deliberately not in this wireframe

Entry popup or interstitial

Google treats intrusive mobile interstitials as a ranking negative, and on an informational page it fires before any trust is built. The inline capture at block 11 does the same job without the cost.

Product block above the fold

It reads as thin commercial content wearing a legal hat — to Google and to the reader. It would put the ranking itself at risk to gain a placement that converts worse than block 03.

Carousels and countdown timers

Carousels hide inventory behind an interaction almost nobody performs. Countdown urgency on a legal-reference page actively contradicts the credibility the page depends on.

Product schema on this page

The page’s main entity is an Article. Marking up the embedded products here competes with your actual product pages for the same rich result. Let the cards link out; keep the markup where the buying happens.
