North Carolina Knife Rules — The Short Version

Four things worth knowing before you carry

North Carolina at a glance Updated 1 September 2026
Blade length
No limitNorth Carolina sets no statutory blade-length restriction. Case law has treated a folder around 4.5″ closed as an ordinary pocket knife.
Open carry
LawfulN.C.G.S. 14-269 restricts concealed carry only. No statute bars open carry of knives generally.
Concealed carry
Pocket-knife testConcealed bowie knives, dirks, daggers and razors are prohibited under 14-269(a). The exception in 14-269(d) covers an ordinary pocket knife carried closed — one that cannot be opened by throwing, explosive or spring action.
Restricted places
Still applyKnives are barred on school property (14-269.2), in courthouses and certain state buildings (14-269.4), and at parades and demonstrations (14-277.2).

Sources: N.C.G.S. 14-269 — Carrying concealed weapons · North Carolina Knife Laws. General information only — Michigan Sports Outdoor is a retailer, not a law firm. Check your city ordinance before you carry.

Automatics are legal to own and to carry openly in North Carolina, but they fall outside the ordinary pocket knife exception — so a spring-opening knife carried concealed is not protected by 14-269(d). If you carry concealed in North Carolina, stay with a conventional folder. Everything in the grid above is one.
