Texas Knife Law — Quick Answers

Updated 1 September 2026 · sourced from the Texas Legislature

Blade length carry limit None Any length is legal to carry

Switchblades & automatics Legal Since 1 September 2013
Daggers, dirks, Bowie knives Legal Category deleted in 2017
Over 5.5″ at a school Felony 2–10 years, up to $10,000
City rules override state? No Texas has knife preemption

What size knife can you carry in Texas?

Any size — Texas sets no blade-length carry limit. A blade over 5.5″ becomes a “location-restricted knife” under Penal Code 46.01, which limits where you can take it, not whether you can carry it.

Source: Texas Penal Code Ch. 46

Are Bowie knives and daggers illegal in Texas?

No — that category no longer exists. House Bill 1935 removed daggers, dirks, stilettos, poniards, swords, spears and Bowie knives from the prohibited list on 1 September 2017.

Source: AKTI — Texas HB 1935

Are switchblades legal in Texas?

Yes. House Bill 1862 removed switchblades from the prohibited-weapons list effective 1 September 2013, so automatics are legal to own, carry, sell and transport in Texas.

Source: AKTI — Texas HB 1862

Where can’t you carry a knife over 5.5″ in Texas?

Schools, bars, sporting events, hospitals, places of worship, amusement parks and correctional facilities. Most breaches are a Class C misdemeanour capped at $500 — but a school is a third-degree felony carrying 2 to 10 years.

Source: Texas Penal Code 46.03

Can a minor carry a knife in Texas?

Under 5.5″, yes — there is no age limit. A location-restricted knife cannot be sold to anyone under 18, and an under-18 may only carry one at home, on their own property or in their own vehicle, unless a parent or guardian is directly supervising.

Source: AKTI — Texas

Can Texas cities make their own knife rules?

No — Texas has statewide knife preemption. Local Government Code 229.001 bars cities from adopting knife rules stricter than state law, with a narrow exception during riots or declared disasters.

Source: Local Gov’t Code 229.001
