Michigan Knife Law — Quick Answers

Updated 1 September 2026 · sourced from the Michigan Legislature

Blade length limit None statewide Detroit & Lansing cap at 3″
Switchblades & automatics Legal Ban repealed 11 October 2017
Folding pocket knives Legal Open or concealed
Concealed dagger or stiletto Felony Up to 5 years, $2,500 fine
Those knives in your car Felony Even locked in the boot
City rules override state? Yes No knife preemption in Michigan

What size knife can you carry in Michigan?

Any length — Michigan sets no statewide blade limit. The exception is city rules: Detroit and Lansing both restrict blades over three inches in public places.

Source: AKTI

Are switchblades legal in Michigan?

Yes. Public Act 96 of 2017 repealed the switchblade ban on 11 October 2017. You can own and carry automatics — just not dagger-shaped ones concealed.

Source: Public Act 96 of 2017

Can you keep a knife in your car in Michigan?

An ordinary folder, yes. A dagger, dirk or stiletto, no. Those are a felony in any vehicle you occupy — reachable or not, boot included.

Source: MCL 750.227(1)

Is there a 3-inch knife law in Michigan?

Not statewide. The three-inch figure in MCL 750.226 only applies when someone carries a weapon intending to use it unlawfully. It is not a carry limit.

Source: MCL 750.226

Which knives are illegal to conceal in Michigan?

Daggers, dirks, stilettos and double-edged non-folding blades. Michigan restricts by blade shape, not by how the knife opens or how long it is.

Source: MCL 750.227(1)

Which Michigan cities restrict knives?

Detroit and Lansing. Both cap public carry at three inches. Michigan has no knife preemption law, so any city can set its own rules.

Source: AKTI
