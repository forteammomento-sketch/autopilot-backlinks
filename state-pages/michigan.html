Michigan Knife Laws (2026): What You Can Legally Own and Carry

Michigan repealed its switchblade ban in 2017 and has no statewide blade-length limit. The real risk is concealed carry — a dagger, dirk or stiletto out of sight is a felony, and that includes one sitting in your vehicle.

What Michigan Actually Prohibits

Every rule below cites the statute it comes from

Concealed carry of stabbing weapons

Felony

It is a felony to carry a dagger, dirk, stiletto, double-edged non-folding stabbing instrument or other dangerous weapon concealed on your person. The penalty is up to five years’ imprisonment, a fine of up to $2,500, or both.

The same prohibition covers possession of those weapons in any vehicle you operate or occupy — and it applies whether or not the knife is within reach. A dagger locked in the trunk is still covered.

MCL 750.227(1)

Carrying any knife with unlawful intent

Felony

Separately, it is an offence to carry a dangerous weapon — including a knife with a blade over three inches — with intent to use it unlawfully against another person.

This is where the widely repeated “three-inch rule” comes from, and it is almost always misquoted. The threshold only matters once unlawful intent is established.

MCL 750.226

Automatic and switchblade knives

Legal

Public Act 96 of 2017 repealed Michigan’s switchblade prohibition, effective 11 October 2017. Adults may lawfully own and possess automatic-opening knives in Michigan.

The repeal did not create a carry exemption. An automatic that also meets the description of a dagger or double-edged stabbing instrument remains subject to the concealed-carry felony above.

Public Act 96 of 2017, repealing MCL 750.226a

Local ordinances — no state preemption

Check your city

Michigan has no knife preemption statute. The state’s preemption law, MCL 123.1102, covers pistols, firearms, pneumatic guns and ammunition — it does not mention knives. A knife preemption bill passed both chambers in 2021 and was vetoed.

Cities may therefore set their own limits. Detroit and Lansing both restrict carrying knives with blades over three inches in public places.

AKTI — Michigan

The mistake almost everyone makes

People assume the 2017 switchblade repeal legalised concealed carry of everything. It did not. Michigan draws its line at the shape and function of the blade — double-edged, non-folding, built to stab — not at how the knife opens. A legal automatic can still be an illegal concealed weapon if its blade is dagger-shaped.
